# Graf Gerhard III.
>[!infobox]  
>**Born**: c. 1292/1293

## Overview
Gerhard III. of Holstein-Rendsburg, commonly known as Gerhard der Große (“Gerhard the Great”), was one of the most powerful members of the Rendsburg line of the House of Schauenburg.

He ruled Holstein-Rendsburg from 1304 until his death in 1340 and became a major political and military power in Schleswig and Denmark during a period of political instability.

For Rendsburg, Gerhard III. is especially important because he confirmed the city’s existing rights and privileges in 1339, granted Rendsburg extensive lands south of the Eider, and gave the city its own jurisdiction. This reduced the direct authority of the castle’s representative over the town.

The original 1339 document confirming Rendsburg’s rights is preserved in the Rendsburg City Archive.
## Background
Gerhard III. was the son of Heinrich I., Count of Holstein-Rendsburg, and Heilwig of Bronckhorst.

His father died in 1304, after which Gerhard inherited the Rendsburg branch of the Holstein territories. He subsequently became one of the most influential Schauenburg rulers.

In 1326, Gerhard acquired the Duchy of Schleswig and became duke. He was also deeply involved in Danish politics and exercised enormous political influence during the Danish succession crisis of the 1330s.

His power eventually extended over large parts of Denmark and Schleswig-Holstein.

Gerhard was married to Sofie of Werle/Mecklenburg. Their sons included Heinrich II. and Nikolaus, who later inherited territories connected to the Rendsburg line.
## Connection to Rendsburg
Gerhard III. is one of the most important medieval rulers in Rendsburg’s history.

In 1339, he issued a document confirming the rights previously granted to Rendsburg and transferred extensive lands south of the Eider to the city.

Most importantly, he granted Rendsburg its own jurisdiction. This meant that the town was no longer under the immediate judicial authority of the castle’s representative.

The document is therefore an important milestone in Rendsburg’s development as a legally independent medieval town.

The exact date recorded for the confirmation is 6 December 1339.

## Military Connection
Gerhard III. was a major military and political power during the conflicts surrounding the Danish crown and the Duchy of Schleswig.

He was involved in several military conflicts during the Danish succession crisis. His victory at Lohheide in 1331 strengthened his position and contributed to the Treaty of Kiel in 1332.

His political power ultimately made him a target of opposition in Denmark.
